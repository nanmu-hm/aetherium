# Aetherium Narrative Emergence

## 1. Purpose

Aetherium separates world simulation from storytelling.

The simulation produces history. The narrative layer observes that history and identifies meaningful causal structures without fabricating events.

    World Simulation
          ↓
    Event History
          ↓
    Causal Graph
          ↓
    Narrative Signals
          ↓
    Story Arc
          ↓
    Novelization

## 2. Event Graph

Every authoritative event becomes a node in a historical graph.

Edges may represent:
- caused_by
- enabled_by
- prevented_by
- reacted_to
- followed_by
- revealed
- escalated
- resolved

The graph allows the system to find chains that are invisible when events are viewed only as a chronological list.

## 3. Narrative Signals

The Narrative Emergence Engine looks for signals such as:
- persistent conflict;
- repeated attempts and failures;
- major reversals;
- unexpected consequences;
- character transformation;
- relationship transformation;
- faction transformation;
- resource or territorial change;
- unresolved promises or threats;
- converging character goals;
- separation and reunion;
- sacrifice and cost;
- discovery of hidden information.

These are signals, not instructions to manufacture drama.

## 4. Narrative Salience

An event becomes narratively significant when it has meaningful causal or character impact.

Possible dimensions:
- number and importance of downstream consequences;
- number of characters affected;
- magnitude of state change;
- persistence of the resulting conflict;
- character value/goal disruption;
- relationship impact;
- faction/world impact;
- degree of unresolved tension.

Salience should be explainable. The system should be able to answer why an event was considered important.

## 5. Emergent Protagonists

Aetherium may detect that a character is becoming central to the history.

Signals can include:
- repeated causal participation;
- strong goal conflict;
- substantial character change;
- influence on other characters;
- influence on world state;
- recurring unresolved choices.

The system may propose an emergent protagonist, but protagonist identity can also be locked by the user.

## 6. Story Arc Formation

A StoryArc can be proposed when a meaningful causal chain becomes coherent.

Typical structure:

    Trigger
      ↓
    Rising pressure
      ↓
    Complications
      ↓
    Reversal
      ↓
    Crisis
      ↓
    Consequence / Resolution

This is a description of discovered history, not a template that the simulator must force characters to follow.

## 7. Completion

An arc can be considered complete when its central causal conflict has reached a meaningful state transition.

Possible completion states:
- resolved;
- transformed;
- abandoned with consequence;
- permanently blocked;
- transferred to a larger conflict.

A complete arc may create the conditions for the next arc.

## 8. Narrative Attractor

A world may have thematic constraints that make certain developments more meaningful.

Examples:
- loyalty versus survival;
- freedom versus order;
- love versus duty;
- ambition versus identity.

A Narrative Attractor should influence what the narrative layer notices and connects. It must not dictate a predetermined plot route.

## 9. Novelization Boundary

Novelization begins only after historical material is sufficiently coherent.

The Writer Agent receives:
- approved StoryArc;
- relevant event chain;
- character states and changes;
- relationship history;
- spatial and chronological constraints;
- canon/provenance information;
- user style requirements.

The Writer Agent may choose scene presentation and prose, but must not silently alter historical facts.

## 10. Human Review

The user can approve, reject, merge, split, rename, or annotate an emergent StoryArc.

Rejecting a narrative interpretation does not erase the underlying world history.

This distinction lets Aetherium explore many stories from the same living world.
